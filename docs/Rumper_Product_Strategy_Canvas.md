# Rumper Product Strategy Canvas

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 28 August 2026  
**Planning horizon:** MVP validation through initial productionization  
**Primary market:** First-time homebuyers in Greater Jakarta (Jabodetabek)  
**Strategy confidence:** Medium — product direction is well documented, but willingness to pay, acquisition economics, and real-world behavioral outcomes still require validation.

---

## Executive Decision

Rumper will compete as the **buyer-side decision layer between browsing a property and paying a booking fee**.

It will not attempt to win through the largest listing inventory, the most map markers, or a universal neighborhood score. Rumper will win by helping buyers:

1. understand which location trade-offs matter to their lives;
2. identify areas worth serious investigation;
3. evaluate a real property using evidence, limitations, and verification actions; and
4. make a more deliberate decision without outsourcing judgment to an algorithm.

The product consists of two connected loops:

| Loop | Role | Core journey |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery Loop** | Acquisition and initial value | Priorities → scenarios → Location DNA → curated areas → shortlist |
| **Decision Readiness Loop** | Deeper value and potential monetization | Property candidate → claims → evidence gaps → verification tasks → readiness brief |

The strategic principle is simple: **discovery attracts the buyer; decision readiness earns trust and creates commercial value.**

## 1. Vision

### Vision statement

Help first-time buyers understand the location consequences of a home before their money becomes difficult to recover.

### Aspirational impact

Rumper aims to make location due diligence a normal part of buying a first home in Greater Jakarta. Buyers should enter surveys, developer conversations, and booking-fee decisions knowing:

- what matters most to their household;
- which compromises they are consciously accepting;
- which claims have credible support;
- which uncertainties remain unresolved; and
- what they should investigate next.

### Values

1. **Evidence over persuasion** — show the basis and limitations of every consequential claim.
2. **Useful uncertainty over artificial certainty** — unknown information becomes an investigation task, not a hidden weakness.
3. **Personal relevance over universal ranking** — a location is evaluated relative to the buyer's daily life.
4. **Materiality over information volume** — prioritize facts capable of changing the decision.
5. **Support judgment without replacing it** — never issue a Buy/Don't Buy verdict.

## 2. Market Segments

Segments are defined by the buyer's problem and decision stage rather than demographics alone.

### Segment 1 — The Active Cautious Commuter

**Priority:** First segment

**Situation**

- Actively searching in Greater Jakarta.
- Knows an approximate budget and one or more daily anchors.
- Has browsed listings or saved possible areas but lacks clear decision criteria.
- Worries about commute, flooding, access reliability, affordability, or hidden location compromises.
- Is likely to purchase within the foreseeable future rather than browsing casually.

**Job to Be Done**

> When I am deciding where to focus my home search, help me understand which locations are realistically compatible with my daily life so I do not waste time surveying unsuitable properties.

**Desired outcome**

A smaller and more intentional shortlist whose trade-offs the buyer can explain.

**Why this segment first**

- The commute-versus-space problem is frequent and easy to represent through scenarios.
- Workplace anchors provide a concrete personalization input.
- The buyer already experiences enough urgency to test a decision tool.
- The initial solution can be validated without comprehensive national data.

### Segment 2 — The Candidate-Stage Buyer

**Priority:** Second, and the strongest potential monetization segment

**Situation**

- Has identified one or more serious property candidates.
- Is evaluating developer or agent claims.
- Is approaching a survey, booking fee, or other financial commitment.
- Uses fragmented sources such as maps, brochures, social media, spreadsheets, and family advice.

**Job to Be Done**

> Before I commit money, help me identify which location claims and risks are supported, limited, or still unverified, and tell me what to investigate next.

**Desired outcome**

A structured investigation plan and a defensible understanding of the property's material uncertainties.

### Segment 3 — The Couple or Family Planner

**Priority:** Later expansion

**Situation**

- Must reconcile multiple workplace, school, healthcare, caregiving, and mobility needs.
- Experiences disagreement that is difficult to express beyond personal opinions.

**Job to Be Done**

> Help our household make conflicting priorities and acceptable compromises visible so we can reach a shared decision without one person's concerns being ignored.

**Desired outcome**

A shared decision model, visible disagreements, and an agreed verification plan.

## 3. Relative Costs

### Competitive cost position

Rumper should compete through **differentiated value**, not the lowest possible cost.

Its intended position is:

- more useful and personalized than a free quiz;
- more decision-oriented than a conventional property portal;
- more structured than informal research across maps, social media, and spreadsheets; and
- more accessible than hiring a specialist consultant for every candidate.

### Cost strategy

1. Begin with narrowly curated Greater Jakarta coverage.
2. Validate customer value before purchasing or building expensive data infrastructure.
3. Build the preference, evidence, and decision workflow internally.
4. Partner or license commodity capabilities such as maps, routing, and external datasets.
5. Add automated data coverage corridor by corridor, based on demonstrated user demand.

### Commercial hypothesis

A plausible model is:

- **Free:** preference discovery, Location DNA, curated-area exploration, and a limited shortlist.
- **Paid:** candidate evaluation, deeper evidence, comparison, verification planning, and shareable decision-readiness briefs.

This model is a hypothesis. Pricing, payment frequency, and willingness to pay need verification before being treated as strategy facts.

## 4. Value Propositions

| Target segment | What before | How Rumper helps | What after | Current alternatives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Active Cautious Commuter | Search criteria are based on assumptions, abstract ratings, or listing filters | Realistic trade-off scenarios create an explainable Location DNA and curated area categories | A focused shortlist with understandable reasons, compromises, and unknowns | Property portals, Google Maps, social media, family advice, spreadsheets |
| Candidate-Stage Buyer | Marketing claims and risk information are scattered and difficult to assess | Rumper structures each issue as Claim → Evidence → Limitation → Action | A prioritized verification plan before financial commitment | Sales agents, developer brochures, manual research, survey notes |
| Couple or Family Planner | Household disagreements remain implicit and emotionally difficult | Rumper keeps preferences separate, shows conflicts, and creates a shared investigation process | A more transparent household decision | Messaging threads, informal discussion, one partner maintaining a spreadsheet |

### Core value proposition

> Rumper helps first-time homebuyers discover their real location priorities and investigate the claims and uncertainties that could change their decision before they commit money.

### Positioning statement

> For first-time homebuyers in Greater Jakarta who are uncertain which locations or properties genuinely fit their lives, Rumper is a buyer-first location discovery and decision-readiness product that turns lifestyle trade-offs and property claims into explainable recommendations and concrete verification actions. Unlike property portals or generic neighborhood rankings, Rumper makes uncertainty visible and never replaces the buyer's judgment with an opaque score.

## 5. Strategic Trade-offs

Focus requires explicit rejection of attractive but distracting opportunities.

### Rumper will not

- become a general property marketplace;
- compete on listing inventory or sponsored placement;
- present universal “best neighborhood” rankings;
- expose a numeric neighborhood-fit or safety score;
- issue Buy/Don't Buy recommendations;
- build a generic AI property-search chatbot;
- optimize for thousands of simultaneous map markers;
- promise proactive alerts before reliable monitoring infrastructure exists;
- claim comprehensive school or crime intelligence without authoritative data;
- expand nationally before demonstrating value in Greater Jakarta; or
- represent curated or mock data as verified production evidence.

### Why these trade-offs strengthen the product

- They protect the buyer-first position from marketplace incentives.
- They reduce false precision and legal or reputational risk.
- They concentrate limited resources on the end-to-end decision workflow.
- They make Rumper's product behavior distinct from listing portals.

### Feature decisions

| Capability | Strategic decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Side-by-side area comparison | **Prioritize now** | Directly improves trade-off clarity and uses the existing curated model. |
| Commute validator | **Next major data capability** | Commute is central to the first segment and can be validated with limited corridor coverage. |
| Visible 0–100 fit score | **Reject** | Implies precision the product cannot support and conflicts with explainability principles. |
| Internal matching calculation | **Allow with guardrails** | May support deterministic categorization, but must never hide a critical disqualifier or be presented as objective truth. |
| Risk and change alerts | **Defer** | Alerts require reliable ingestion, materiality rules, provenance, and operational monitoring. |
| AI conversational listing search | **Reject for current strategy** | Pulls Rumper toward a generic property portal. |
| Evidence-grounded investigation assistant | **Explore after evidence infrastructure** | Can help users interrogate known evidence, limitations, and next actions without inventing facts. |
| High-scale listing map | **Defer** | Breadth of markers does not improve the current decision outcome. |
| School and crime intelligence | **Defer pending demand and data quality** | Both require careful sourcing, interpretation, and local context. |

## 6. Key Metrics

### North Star Metric — Material Decision Clarity Rate

**Definition:** The percentage of qualified buyers who resolve at least one decision-changing uncertainty and can identify a concrete next action.

This includes outcomes such as:

- adding an area to a serious shortlist;
- removing an area because a material compromise became clear;
- verifying or challenging a property claim;
- creating a site-survey action; or
- identifying a major unresolved risk before paying a booking fee.

This metric values informed rejection as well as continued interest.

### Quarterly OMTM — Validated Core Loop Rate

**Definition:** The percentage of test users who:

1. complete preference discovery;
2. save at least one area;
3. accurately explain one personal trade-off; and
4. identify at least one unresolved question without prompting.

### Supporting metrics

- Preference Discovery Completion Rate
- Trade-off Clarity Improvement
- Recommendation Explainability
- Serious Shortlist Creation Rate
- Search Reframing Rate
- Map-to-Candidate Conversion
- Material Uncertainty Resolution Rate
- Verification Task Completion Rate
- Decision Readiness Continuation Rate
- Evidence Citation Open Rate
- User-reported trust in conclusions and limitations

### Metrics Rumper will not optimize

- swipe count;
- session count;
- time spent;
- artificial streaks or levels;
- number of map markers viewed; or
- property purchase conversion.

## 7. Growth Strategy

### Growth model

Start with **product-led and content-led growth**, supported by focused partnerships.

### Acquisition channels

1. Search and social content addressing specific buyer anxieties:
   - realistic Jabodetabek commute trade-offs;
   - flood and access investigation;
   - questions to ask before paying a booking fee;
   - how to compare two seemingly similar locations.
2. Shareable Location DNA and area-comparison outputs.
3. First-time buyer communities.
4. Partnerships with mortgage advisers, buyer advocates, employers, and housing education programs.
5. Referral moments built around shared decision briefs rather than generic invitations.

### Growth loop

Useful educational content → guided discovery → shortlist or candidate evaluation → shareable brief → household or adviser collaboration → new qualified users.

### Scaling approach

1. Prove the workflow with a small number of Greater Jakarta corridors.
2. Identify which data categories most often change decisions.
3. Automate only those categories.
4. Expand coverage based on demand and evidence availability.

### Unit economics questions requiring validation

- What acquisition channels produce active buyers rather than casual browsers?
- Does candidate-stage urgency create willingness to pay?
- Is the preferred payment model per property, subscription, or partner-sponsored access?
- What is the marginal data and support cost per evaluated candidate?
- How often does a buyer evaluate more than one property?

## 8. Required Capabilities

### Build internally

- Scenario and preference-signal design
- Explainable Location DNA model
- Deterministic category matching with visible rationale
- Shortlist and side-by-side comparison
- Claim → Evidence → Limitation → Action model
- Verification-task and readiness workflow
- Evidence provenance and freshness model
- User-controlled corrections and overrides
- Product analytics focused on decision outcomes
- Trust, accessibility, and uncertainty UX

### Partner or license

- Maps and geocoding
- Historical routing and commute estimates
- Transit information
- Official flood and hazard datasets
- Pricing and market reference data
- Verified public-facility and school information

### Operational capabilities to develop

- Data-source evaluation and licensing
- Geographic quality assurance
- Claim review and correction process
- Dataset freshness monitoring
- Incident response for incorrect or outdated evidence
- Editorial review for high-stakes explanations
- Clear separation of observed fact, inference, user report, and unknown information

### Do not build yet

- A proprietary foundation model
- Nationwide geospatial infrastructure
- A property-listing supply operation
- Real-time alerting across many risk categories
- Automated crime or school rankings

## 9. Can't/Won't — Defensibility

### Current reality

Rumper does not yet have a strong defensible moat. Its interface, mascot, scenarios, and categorical matching can be copied.

### Potential defensibility

1. **Longitudinal decision context** — a buyer's evolving preferences, overrides, shortlist logic, evidence, and verification history.
2. **Local decision ontology** — structured knowledge about which Greater Jakarta location factors and evidence gaps materially affect different buyers.
3. **Verification workflow data** — learning which questions, evidence, and field checks actually resolve uncertainty.
4. **Trust and provenance** — a reputation for showing source, freshness, limitations, and unknowns consistently.
5. **Household switching costs** — shared decision history and evidence become increasingly valuable as a household evaluates multiple candidates.
6. **Buyer-aligned brand** — a credible refusal to use developer sponsorship to manipulate rankings or conclusions.

### Strategic “won't” advantage

Property marketplaces may be commercially reluctant to emphasize uncertainty, hidden compromises, or reasons not to proceed because their incentives favor inquiries and transactions. Rumper can differentiate by remaining structurally buyer-aligned.

This advantage only exists if revenue sources do not compromise the product's independence.

## Strategy Coherence Check

| Strategic element | Reinforcing relationship |
|---|---|
| First segment | Commute-sensitive active buyers provide a concrete and urgent starting problem. |
| Discovery Loop | Builds personal context and creates an initial shortlist. |
| Decision Readiness Loop | Applies the same context at the moment of financial risk. |
| Categorical recommendations | Preserve explainability and avoid false precision. |
| Narrow geographic coverage | Makes evidence quality and operational learning manageable. |
| Content-led acquisition | Attracts buyers already researching material uncertainties. |
| Paid candidate evaluation | Monetizes deeper decision support rather than listings or rankings. |
| Defensibility | Accumulates through trusted decision history and verification knowledge. |

### Current incoherence requiring correction

The current product implementation contains numeric location-risk scores and hard-coded language implying official evidence. These conflict with the documented commitments to no false precision and explicit uncertainty.

Before expanding the feature roadmap, Rumper should:

1. distinguish clearly between **fit**, **risk exposure**, **evidence strength**, and **decision readiness**;
2. replace unexplained `/100` outputs with categorical states and supporting evidence;
3. label all prototype data visibly as illustrative;
4. prohibit claims such as “anti-hallucination” until the assistant is retrieval-backed, source-grounded, and capable of refusing unsupported answers; and
5. maintain a capability ledger distinguishing UI prototype, functional interaction, mock-backed feature, integrated service, and production-verified capability.

## Critical Strategic Hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Risk if false |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Active buyers experience location trade-off confusion strongly enough to use a guided discovery product. | The discovery loop becomes an interesting quiz rather than a decision tool. |
| H2 | Scenario choices reveal priorities more accurately than direct preference ratings. | Location DNA adds complexity without improving decisions. |
| H3 | Buyers trust categorical explanations and visible limitations more than numeric rankings. | Rumper's differentiated presentation may feel less decisive than alternatives. |
| H4 | Buyers will carry Location DNA into evaluation of a real candidate. | The two product loops remain disconnected. |
| H5 | Resolving evidence gaps before a booking fee creates willingness to pay. | The readiness workflow may create value but not a sustainable business. |
| H6 | Commute and flood/access uncertainty are the best initial data categories. | Early data investment may target the wrong decision factors. |
| H7 | A buyer-aligned trust position can attract users without marketplace inventory. | Customer acquisition may be too expensive or infrequent. |

## Low-Effort Validation Experiments

### Experiment 1 — Concierge core-loop test

- Recruit 10–15 active first-time buyers.
- Run the discovery flow and manually prepare a shortlist explanation.
- Ask users to explain their top trade-off and one remaining uncertainty without prompts.
- Measure changes to search criteria and planned survey behavior.

**Tests:** H1, H2, H3

### Experiment 2 — Real candidate readiness brief

- Ask 5–10 buyers to submit a property they are seriously considering.
- Manually create a Claim → Evidence → Limitation → Action brief.
- Observe whether the brief changes questions asked, survey plans, or booking-fee timing.
- Test willingness to pay only after delivering the outcome.

**Tests:** H4, H5

### Experiment 3 — Data-category prioritization

- Present buyers with potential evidence modules: commute, flood, access reliability, pricing, schools, crime, and planned infrastructure.
- Require them to choose which two could materially change their decision.
- Compare stated priorities with actual questions and actions during candidate evaluation.

**Tests:** H6

### Experiment 4 — Categorical versus numeric comprehension test

- Use research-only prototypes, not production interfaces.
- Compare a `/100` presentation with categorical evidence and explicit limitations.
- Measure comprehension, perceived certainty, trust, and ability to identify unresolved risk.

**Tests:** H3

### Experiment 5 — Acquisition-message test

- Test landing-page messages focused on:
  1. finding suitable areas;
  2. avoiding location regret;
  3. checking claims before paying a booking fee.
- Measure qualified starts and candidate submissions, not raw clicks.

**Tests:** H7 and segment urgency

## Recommended Sequence

### Now — Strategic and product integrity

1. Reconcile scoring behavior with product principles.
2. Add explicit prototype-data disclosures.
3. Remove unsupported official-source and anti-hallucination claims.
4. Create a capability ledger for every major feature.
5. Instrument the Validated Core Loop Rate.

### Next — Validate the core outcome

1. Test discovery with active buyers.
2. Complete shortlist and comparison usability.
3. Test real candidate-readiness briefs manually.
4. Verify which evidence categories change decisions.

### Then — Productionize narrow evidence

1. Historical commute ranges for selected anchors and corridors.
2. Flood and access evidence with provenance and limitations.
3. Persistent candidate and verification workflows.
4. Shareable household decision brief.

### Later — Expand only after evidence of demand

1. Couple Sync and shared household decisions.
2. Evidence-grounded investigation assistant.
3. Additional area and data coverage.
4. Material change alerts.
5. School, infrastructure, or other specialized intelligence.
