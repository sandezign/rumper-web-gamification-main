# Rumper Visual Identity System Specification

**Brand:** Rumper  
**Category:** Buyer-side location risk intelligence / PropTech decision support  
**Visual North Star:** **The Calm Evidence Map**  
**Primary Brand Character:** Buyer-first · Calm · Evidence-led · Precise · Transparent  
**Primary Use Contexts:** Web application, location-risk workspace, marketing website, preliminary Mini Check reports, PDF reports, mobile web, presentation and social communication assets.

> **Logo direction status:** The supplied brand documentation establishes Rumper’s visual character, color system, typography, and design principles, but does not contain a sufficiently detailed description of the approved logo geometry. Logo construction, exact proportions, and logo-specific clear-space measurements therefore remain a **designer judgment / approval item** rather than being invented in this specification.

---

# 1. Brand Identity Principle

Rumper should visually behave less like a property marketplace and more like an **independent evidence layer around a consequential purchase decision**.

The identity should communicate:

- analytical credibility without looking institutional or bureaucratic;
- calmness without becoming passive;
- independence without looking adversarial;
- technical precision without becoming a GIS or engineering product;
- approachability for first-time buyers without appearing playful or simplistic.

The identity should reinforce Rumper's product position as a buyer-side location-risk adviser whose value comes from traceable evidence, confidence labels, deterministic safeguards, visible red flags, and concrete validation actions—not from an unexplained “AI score.” 
## Core Visual Principle

**Evidence has authority. Brand expression creates recognition. Neither should overpower the other.**

Rumper Green therefore acts primarily as an **identity and action color**, while risk, evidence quality, and confidence use their own semantic system.

**Judgment call:** Final approved logo artwork is required before lockup ratios can be numerically frozen.

# 3. Logo Clear Space

Define the logo clear-space unit as **X**, where:

**X = height of the dominant lowercase character body in the Rumper wordmark.**

Minimum exclusion area:

- Top: **1X**
- Bottom: **1X**
- Left: **1X**
- Right: **1X**

For highly visible brand moments such as covers or landing-page hero areas, prefer **1.5X–2X**.

No text, border, map marker, icon, photograph edge, UI control, or decorative element should enter this area.

### Symbol-Only Clear Space

Use at least **25% of the symbol width** on every side.

# 5. Forbidden Logo Uses

Never:

- stretch, compress, skew, or distort the mark;
- change the relationship between symbol and wordmark;
- recolor the logo using semantic warning, danger, or evidence colors;
- use gradients unless explicitly incorporated into the approved master artwork;
- add strokes, shadows, glows, bevels, highlights, or 3D effects;
- rotate the logo;
- place the full-color logo over low-contrast green backgrounds;
- place the logo directly over visually busy photography;
- place the logo over detailed map tiles without a controlled background surface;
- recreate the wordmark using typed text;
- use Rumper Green to imply that a property is safe or verified;
- place the logo inside arbitrary decorative shapes;
- crop the mark as a stylistic effect.

The existing design system explicitly reserves Rumper Green for identity and action rather than safety or evidence quality.

# 10. Color Usage Principles

Recommended visual proportion for general brand communication:

**60–70%** white / reading neutrals  
**20–30%** deep teal / typography / framing  
**5–10%** Rumper Green and supporting accents

This is not a rigid mathematical layout rule. It is intended to prevent excessive brand-green usage.

## The Action, Not Safety Rule

Rumper Green means:

- Rumper;
- primary action;
- active branded interaction.

It does **not** mean:

- safe;
- recommended;
- low risk;
- verified;
- strong evidence;
- successful score.

This distinction is fundamental to the trust model.

# 11. Accessibility

All digital identity applications should meet **WCAG AA minimum contrast requirements**.

Minimum targets:

**Normal text:** 4.5:1  
**Large text:** 3:1  
**UI graphics and interactive boundaries:** 3:1 where applicable.

Never depend on color alone for:

- risk;
- evidence quality;
- confidence;
- selected states;
- warnings;
- errors.

Use supporting:

- text;
- iconography;
- labels;
- patterns;
- stroke treatment.

Rumper's product specification also requires visible focus rings, reduced-motion support, minimum 44 px touch targets, and non-color indicators for consequential status.

# 17. Typographic Behaviour

Rumper typography should be:

- sentence case by default;
- concise;
- left aligned in information-rich contexts;
- moderately weighted;
- readable at mobile sizes;
- structured rather than decorative.

Avoid:

- uppercase headlines;
- compressed type;
- high-contrast serif display typography;
- dramatic danger typography;
- text gradients;
- excessive bolding;
- editorial luxury-estate styling.

# 18. Photography Direction

Photography should reinforce **real decision context**, not aspirational property selling.

## Rule 1 — Buyer Perspective

Prefer scenes that suggest the buyer is observing or evaluating a place.

Examples:

- street access;
- neighborhood entrances;
- road conditions;
- transit connections;
- surrounding environment;
- buyer reviewing information during a location visit.

## Rule 2 — Documentary Over Promotional

Photography should feel:

- observational;
- natural;
- grounded;
- geographically credible.

Avoid glossy real-estate photography whose main purpose is to make a property look desirable.

## Rule 3 — Show Context

Environmental context matters more than architectural glamour.

Whenever possible, include:

- street relationship;
- pedestrian access;
- drainage;
- surrounding buildings;
- traffic context;
- transportation infrastructure;
- public facilities.

## Rule 4 — Moderate Color Treatment

Use:

- realistic colors;
- restrained saturation;
- normal dynamic range.

Avoid:

- cinematic teal-orange grading;
- excessively warm aspirational grading;
- HDR-heavy processing;
- dramatic storm imagery solely for emotional effect.

## Rule 5 — Human Presence Should Be Natural

When people are shown:

- candid or semi-documentary composition;
- everyday clothing;
- normal buyer behaviour;
- realistic Indonesian urban context.

Avoid staged stock-photo expressions of fear, surprise, or exaggerated financial anxiety.

---

# 19. Photography to Avoid

Do not use:

- generic smiling-agent handshakes;
- keys handed over in front of a dream house;
- exaggerated “happy family with new home” stock imagery;
- drone photos solely designed to make developments look premium;
- luxury interiors unrelated to location risk;
- dramatic disaster photography for routine risk communication;
- flooded-house images used as visual fear tactics;
- generic AI futuristic city scenes;
- fake dashboards composited into photos;
- seller/developer campaign photography without clear editorial context.

Rumper's product language explicitly rejects alarmism and seller-led framing.

# 20. Illustration Style

Illustration is optional and should not replace evidence.

Recommended style:

**Structured geospatial abstraction.**

Characteristics:

- simple vector geometry;
- restrained line work;
- map-inspired grids;
- routes and points;
- layered translucent evidence zones;
- limited perspective;
- rounded but controlled geometry;
- low visual noise.

Illustration should explain concepts such as:

- evidence layers;
- journey / commute;
- location checks;
- uncertainty;
- validation steps;
- multiple signals converging on one decision.

## Illustration Color Rules

Base:

- Deep Evidence Teal
- Supporting Teal
- white
- Reading Surface

Accents:

- Rumper Green for action/brand;
- muted semantic colors only where their actual meaning is relevant.

---

# 21. Illustration Restrictions

Do not use:

- cartoon houses;
- giant checkmarks suggesting guaranteed safety;
- playful mascots for serious risk findings;
- abstract blobs;
- neon gradients;
- generic AI brains;
- floating holographic maps;
- exaggerated disaster icons;
- 3D property renders as evidence;
- purely ornamental illustration inside serious report sections.

---

# 22. Iconography

Icon style should be:

- simple;
- geometric;
- slightly rounded;
- primarily outline-based;
- optically consistent;
- readable at 16–24 px.

Recommended stroke:

**1.5–2 px**, depending on icon size.

Typical icon categories:

- flood;
- commute;
- physical access;
- facilities;
- environment;
- evidence;
- map;
- source;
- validation;
- warning;
- checklist.

Important statuses should always combine:

**Icon + label + semantic treatment**

rather than icon or color alone.

# 23. Brand Graphic Language

## 23.1 Evidence Layers

A core graphic device may consist of overlapping structured layers that visually suggest:

**Location → Signal → Evidence → Interpretation → Action**

Recommended treatment:

- thin map-like line structures;
- cropped panels;
- rings or boundaries;
- translucent overlays;
- points connected to concise annotation.

Do not make this look like an engineering GIS application.

---

# 24. Evidence Connector Line

A thin connector can visually associate:

- a finding;
- its evidence;
- a point on a map;
- the resulting action.

Recommended characteristics:

- 1 px neutral or supporting teal;
- small circular anchors;
- optional dashed treatment for uncertain evidence;
- solid treatment for stronger linkage.

This supports the product direction in which map evidence and report findings remain synchronized.

# 28. Pattern System

Rumper does not require a highly decorative brand pattern.

If a repeatable graphic texture is needed, use a restrained **Evidence Grid**.

## Evidence Grid Construction

Components:

- faint map/grid lines;
- isolated coordinates or point markers;
- occasional route segments;
- controlled variation in opacity;
- large areas of negative space.

Recommended uses:

- presentation cover;
- report divider;
- social media template;
- website hero background;
- branded documentation.

Recommended opacity:

**3–10%**, depending on background.

The pattern must never resemble actual location evidence unless the underlying geography is genuine and clearly identified.

---

# 29. Brand Motion

Where motion is used digitally, it should feel:

- purposeful;
- short;
- precise;
- reassuring.

Suitable motions:

- evidence card expands;
- map evidence highlights;
- finding-to-map connection animates;
- score explanation appears progressively;
- validation checklist state changes.

Avoid:

- bouncing icons;
- confetti;
- celebratory score animations;
- dramatic flashing warnings;
- excessive map zoom choreography.

The product should also respect reduced-motion preferences.

---

# 30. Report Identity

Reports are among Rumper's most important trust surfaces.

The visual hierarchy should prioritize:

1. critical findings;
2. material limitations;
3. evidence confidence;
4. score;
5. category interpretation;
6. next action;
7. sources;
8. disclaimer.

Critical red flags must appear before aggregate scoring and must never be visually neutralized by a high score.

The current output remains a **preliminary Mini Check**, not a final certification or guaranteed recommendation.

---

# 31. Marketing Identity

Marketing may be more expressive than the product interface, but it must preserve the same trust logic.

Good brand statements should feel like:

- clarity before commitment;
- evidence before assumptions;
- know what to verify;
- buyer-side perspective;
- understand the location, not just the listing.

Avoid visual or verbal framing that suggests:

- “approved property”;
- certification;
- guaranteed safety;
- absolute accuracy;
- magical AI knowledge;
- Rumper chooses the house for the buyer.

The buyer remains the decision-maker.

---

# 32. Brand Voice Alignment

All user-facing product communication should remain in Bahasa Indonesia and follow a tone that is:

- calm;
- factual;
- precise;
- evidence-aware;
- transparent about uncertainty.

Do not use alarmist language such as:

“SANGAT BAHAYA”

or absolute promises such as:

“DIJAMIN BEBAS BANJIR.”

The UX guidelines specifically require Rumper to disclose evidence gaps and attach meaningful validation actions to material findings.

## Product UI

Brand expression should remain restrained.

Evidence hierarchy > visual branding.

## Marketing Website

Brand may become more expressive through:

- deep-teal framing;
- large typography;
- Rumper Green CTA;
- evidence-inspired graphics.

## Reports

Highest emphasis on credibility, readability, provenance, and decision safety.

## Social Media

Use:

- one strong insight;
- one graphic concept;
- strong negative space;
- clear Rumper signature.

Avoid property-listing collage aesthetics.

## Presentation Material

Deep teal and white should form the base system.

Use Rumper Green for:

- key emphasis;
- action;
- small markers.

Do not make every chart green.

---

# 34. Identity Do's

- Use Deep Evidence Teal as the primary authority color.
- Use white and near-white surfaces generously.
- Keep Rumper Green scarce enough that actions remain obvious.
- Treat evidence labels as a separate semantic system.
- Use real environmental context rather than promotional property imagery.
- Maintain DM Sans across major brand communication.
- Communicate uncertainty visibly.
- Keep report and product interfaces calm even when risks are serious.
- Prioritize clarity over decoration.
- Preserve buyer-side independence visually as well as verbally.

---

# 35. Identity Don'ts

- Do not mimic property marketplace aesthetics.
- Do not use generic house-roof logo clichés as secondary graphics.
- Do not convert the brand into an environmental “green = safe” system.
- Do not present Rumper as an AI assistant brand.
- Do not build a red-heavy “risk command center.”
- Do not use gradients as the default brand expression.
- Do not use speculative map data as decoration.
- Do not use excessive rounded blobs.
- Do not turn risk scoring into a game-like success meter.
- Do not make confidence look like a safety rating.
- Do not obscure evidence gaps.
- Do not let premium or marketing surfaces imply stronger certainty than the product actually provides.

# 37. Recommended Identity Hierarchy

When there is tension between visual expression and product trust, resolve it in this order:

**1. Decision safety**  
**2. Evidence legibility**  
**3. Accessibility**  
**4. Brand consistency**  
**5. Visual distinctiveness**  
**6. Decorative expression**

This hierarchy reflects Rumper's current product direction: the Mini Check exists to help first-time buyers understand what is known, uncertain, risky, and worth validating before making a financial commitment.

---

# 38. Final Creative Principle

> **Rumper should look like an independent expert has calmly organized the evidence for you—not like a marketplace trying to sell the property, and not like an algorithm trying to make the decision for you.**

The strongest identity system will therefore be recognizable without becoming loud: **deep teal establishes trust, white creates space for evidence, Rumper Green signals the brand and the next action, while structured typography and map-inspired graphic language make complexity understandable.**
